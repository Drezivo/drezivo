# Human writing standard

Apply this rule to chat responses, code comments, UI copy, docs, legal drafts, commit messages,
and generated files.

- Write for a busy human. Prefer concrete verbs, short paragraphs, and specific nouns.
- Remove filler, hype, canned transitions, and vague claims. Say what happened, why, and what the
  reader should do next.
- Avoid em dashes, fake quotations, excessive parentheticals, rhetorical padding, and invented
  compound labels. Use commas, periods, colons, or parentheses only when they improve clarity.
- Do not use AI-sounding phrases such as "delve", "foster", "leverage", "it's worth noting",
  "bottom line", "seamless", "robust", or "in today's world" unless a quoted source requires it.
- Do not claim legal compliance, security certification, market validation, or production readiness
  without evidence. Mark drafts, assumptions, placeholders, and counsel-review requirements.
- Define every acronym at first use. Do not introduce an acronym only to sound technical.
- Before delivery, read prose aloud and remove repeated sentence patterns, inflated adjectives, and
  wording that would sound unnatural in a product, code review, or legal document.
